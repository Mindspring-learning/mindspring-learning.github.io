1. Big picture: what is a system of particles?

A collection of particles can often be studied as a whole, instead of tracking every particle separately.

System of particles

A system is a collection of two or more particles considered together. The particles may interact with one another and may experience external forces.

Key idea: Internal forces act between particles within the system. External forces come from outside the chosen system.

Centre of mass (COM)

The centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position of a system. It is a useful point for describing the translational motion of the whole system.

r⃗COM = (Σ mᵢ r⃗ᵢ) / (Σ mᵢ)

Translation vs rotation

Translation: the position of the whole body changes. Rotation: the body turns about an axis. A rigid body can translate and rotate at the same time.

Rigid body model

A rigid body is an idealised body in which the distance between every pair of particles remains constant. Real objects can deform, but the model is useful when deformation is negligible.

2. Centre of mass (COM)

The position depends on both where the masses are and how much mass each particle has.

Two particles on a straight line

xCOM = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂) / (m₁ + m₂)

If the origin is at the first mass, with the second mass a distance d away:

xCOM = (m₂d) / (m₁ + m₂)

The COM lies closer to the heavier mass. For a continuous body, the same idea is extended using integration or symmetry.

Worked example

Two point masses, 2 kg at x = 0 m and 6 kg at x = 4 m.

xCOM = [(2 × 0) + (6 × 4)] / (2 + 6)xCOM = 24 / 8 = 3 m

The centre of mass is 3 m from the origin, closer to the 6 kg mass.

Motion of the centre of mass

P⃗ = M V⃗COMF⃗external = dP⃗/dt = M A⃗COM

Here M is the total mass. For a system of constant total mass, the centre of mass accelerates according to the net external force.

Conservation of linear momentum

If the net external force on a system is zero, its total linear momentum remains constant.

P⃗initial = P⃗final

Internal forces can change individual particles' momenta, but they do not change the total momentum of an isolated system.

3. Torque: the turning effect of a force

A force can produce translation, rotation, or both, depending on where and how it acts.

Definition

τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗|τ| = r F sin θ = F × perpendicular lever arm

Here r is the displacement from the chosen axis/pivot to the point where force acts, F is force, and θ is the angle between r⃗ and F⃗.

SI unit: newton metre (N m). Torque has the same dimensions as energy, but torque and energy are different physical quantities.

How to increase torque?

  • Apply a larger force.
  • Apply it farther from the pivot.
  • Apply it more nearly perpendicular to the lever arm.
This is why a door handle is placed far from the hinges.

Couple

A couple consists of two equal, opposite and parallel forces acting along different lines. Their net force is zero, but they produce a turning effect.

Couple torque = F × perpendicular distance between forces

Rotational equilibrium

For rotational equilibrium, the net external torque about the chosen point is zero.

Στ⃗ = 0

For complete static equilibrium, both net force and net torque must be zero.

ΣF⃗ = 0 and Στ⃗ = 0

4. Angular momentum

The rotational counterpart of linear momentum.

Particle angular momentum

L⃗ = r⃗ × p⃗|L| = r p sin θ

For a particle moving in a circle with velocity perpendicular to the radius, L = mvr.

Torque and angular momentum

τ⃗external = dL⃗/dt

If the net external torque on a system is zero, total angular momentum remains constant.

L⃗initial = L⃗final

Example idea: when a rotating skater pulls their arms inward, their moment of inertia decreases and angular speed increases, if external torque is negligible.

5. Moment of inertia and rotational dynamics

Moment of inertia tells us how strongly a body resists a change in its angular motion about a particular axis.

Moment of inertia

I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ²

For point masses, each mass is multiplied by the square of its perpendicular distance from the axis. SI unit: kg m².

It depends on the mass distribution and the axis chosen, not just on total mass.

Rotational form of Newton's second law

τnet = Iα

For a rigid body rotating about a fixed principal axis with constant I, net torque equals moment of inertia multiplied by angular acceleration α.

SI unit of angular acceleration: rad s⁻².

Rotational kinetic energy

Krot = ½ Iω²

ω is angular speed in rad s⁻¹. A rolling body may have both translational and rotational kinetic energy.

Ktotal = ½ MvCOM² + ½ ICOMω²

Radius of gyration

I = Mk² ⇒ k = √(I/M)

Radius of gyration k is the distance from the axis at which the whole mass M could be imagined concentrated to give the same moment of inertia.

Common standard results to learn

Body and axisMoment of inertia
Thin ring, through centre and perpendicular to planeI = MR²
Solid disc, through centre and perpendicular to planeI = ½MR²
Solid cylinder, about its symmetry axisI = ½MR²
Solid sphere, about a diameterI = ⅖MR²
Thin spherical shell, about a diameterI = ⅔MR²
Thin rod of length L, through centre and perpendicular to rodI = ML²/12
Thin rod of length L, through one end and perpendicular to rodI = ML²/3

These are standard ideal-body results. Always check the axis specified in the question.

Parallel-axis theorem

I = ICOM + Md²

I is the moment of inertia about an axis parallel to an axis through the centre of mass; d is the separation between the axes.

Perpendicular-axis theorem

Iz = Ix + Iy

For a plane lamina lying in the xy-plane, the moment of inertia about the perpendicular z-axis through a point equals the sum about two perpendicular in-plane axes through that point.

7. Solved numerical: torque and angular acceleration

A model example showing the usual exam method.

Question: A constant net torque of 6 N m acts on a body with moment of inertia 2 kg m² about a fixed axis. Find its angular acceleration.

Step 1 — Write the relation:

τnet = Iα

Step 2 — Rearrange:

α = τnet / I

Step 3 — Substitute:

α = 6 / 2 = 3 rad s⁻²

Answer: The angular acceleration is 3 rad s⁻². Check that torque and moment of inertia are in SI units.
