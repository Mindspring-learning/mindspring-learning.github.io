📘 NCERT Learning Notes

Study the concepts first, then test your understanding.

1. What is a cell?

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made of cells or their products.

Remember: The cell is the smallest unit capable of carrying out the essential activities of life.

2. Cell theory

  • All living organisms are composed of cells and cell products.
  • All cells arise from pre-existing cells.
  • Matthias Schleiden studied plants; Theodor Schwann studied animals.
  • Rudolf Virchow later popularised the idea that cells arise from pre-existing cells.

3. Types of organisms

  • Unicellular: one cell performs all life functions; e.g. Amoeba.
  • Multicellular: many cells perform specialised functions; e.g. humans.
  • Cells differ in shape, size and function.

4. Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cells

FeatureProkaryoticEukaryotic
NucleusNo membrane-bound nucleus; DNA lies in a nucleoidMembrane-bound nucleus
Membrane-bound organellesAbsentPresent
Ribosomes70S80S in cytoplasm; 70S in mitochondria and plastids
ExamplesBacteria, cyanobacteriaPlants, animals, fungi, protists

S values are sedimentation coefficients, not ordinary additive units.

5. Plasma membrane

A selectively permeable boundary around the cell. It regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell.

  • Made mainly of lipids and proteins.
  • The fluid mosaic model was proposed by Singer and Nicolson.
  • Passive transport does not directly require cellular energy; active transport requires energy.

6. Cell wall

Plant cells have a cell wall outside the plasma membrane. It provides support, protection and shape.

  • Plant cell walls contain cellulose.
  • The middle lamella is rich in calcium pectate.
  • Plasmodesmata connect neighbouring plant cells.

7. Endoplasmic reticulum

  • Rough ER: ribosomes attached; involved in protein synthesis and processing.
  • Smooth ER: lipid synthesis and other metabolic functions.
  • Forms an extensive network of membranes in the cytoplasm.

8. Golgi apparatus

Consists of flattened membrane-bound sacs called cisternae.

  • Modifies, packages and sorts proteins and lipids.
  • Helps form lysosomes.
  • Has cis and trans faces associated with receiving and dispatching materials.

9. Lysosomes and vacuoles

  • Lysosomes: contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion.
  • Vacuoles: storage compartments enclosed by the tonoplast.
  • The large central vacuole helps maintain turgor in plant cells.

10. Mitochondria

  • Site of aerobic respiration and major ATP production.
  • Have outer and inner membranes; inner folds are cristae.
  • Contain their own DNA and ribosomes.
  • Often called the powerhouse of the cell.

11. Plastids

  • Chloroplasts: photosynthesis.
  • Chromoplasts: contain pigments other than chlorophyll.
  • Leucoplasts: storage of food materials.
  • Chloroplasts contain thylakoids arranged into grana, embedded in stroma.

12. Ribosomes

Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis.

  • Prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S.
  • Eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S.
  • Ribosomes may occur freely in cytoplasm or attached to rough ER.

13. Nucleus

  • Contains genetic material and regulates many cellular activities.
  • The nuclear envelope has two membranes and pores.
  • Chromatin condenses into chromosomes during cell division.
  • The nucleolus is associated with ribosomal RNA production and ribosome assembly.

14. Cytoskeleton and centrosome

  • The cytoskeleton includes microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments.
  • It supports cell shape, movement and intracellular transport.
  • Animal cells commonly have a centrosome with a pair of centrioles.
NEET revision tip: Learn each organelle's structure, function, membrane count and distinguishing feature. Check the current NCERT textbook for the exact prescribed scope.
